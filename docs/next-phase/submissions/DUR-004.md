## 1. Interface and lifecycle

`lib/goal-executor.mjs` exposes `createGoalExecutor({ store, goalId, engine, ... })`
and `GoalAttemptSession`, plus `engineTurn` and `StaleGoalAttemptError`. The engine
adapter is intentionally narrow:

- a turn receives `{ messages, context, goal, generation, budget, tools, signal }`;
- it may return bounded `{ content, toolCalls, status?, aborted?, timedOut?,
budgetExhausted? }`, or be a function with the same request;
- it is never given a store, lock, lease, approval token or writable workspace.

`createGoalExecutor` owns one attempt lifetime. `resume()` performs the accepted
store handshake under the real workspace lock, refuses an uncommitted journal tail,
an active foreign lease, terminal goals and unresolved DUR-003 transaction artifacts,
then transitions `ready`/`needs-attention`/`interrupted` through
`running`/`reconciling`. Provider turns and tool calls each use the accepted durable
sequence: validate authority and preconditions, reserve in the ledger, append and
sync the intent, append the dispatch, perform the effect, append the receipt, then
expose completion. A pause/cancel/terminal transition clears the lease; a pause,
cancel, generation change or closed session makes `GoalAttemptSession` refuse before
the store is called.

`lib/goal-budget.mjs` is pure: it validates counters and reservations, computes
remaining capacity, produces conservative provider/tool reservations with maximum
active time, validates explicit upward amendments against the frozen maximum, and
builds actionable stop/refusal reasons. `lib/goal-context.mjs` builds one bounded
system+user request from trusted criteria, receipt-backed summaries, re-verified
facts, unresolved operations, human-review descriptions and the persisted ledger.
It never emits tool-role messages or a persisted transcript, so there are no orphan
tool replies to reconstruct.

## 2. Requirement mapping

1. **Explicit transitions and durable boundaries.** `GoalAttemptSession.commit`
   re-opens the workspace lock, re-reads persisted state, and checks the attempt
   generation and lease before every append/transition. Provider and tool
   dispatches are separate durable operations; the engine cannot write around them.
   Test: `executor completes only when current artifact evidence exists`,
   `stale attempt authority cannot append even with a fresh workspace lock`.
2. **Reservations before dispatch, cumulative across retries and resume.**
   `dispatchProvider`, `dispatchEdit`, `dispatchTest` and generic tools append and
   sync an intent (which reserves through the accepted store) before the dispatch;
   the store rejects a reservation beyond a cap. Provider success consumes observed
   elapsed active time; provider interruption/timeout/failure keeps the full
   reservation and records `unknown-usage`. A retry, restart or fresh process sees
   the same monotonic used/reserved totals. `budget-exhausted` cannot be resumed
   without an explicit amendment; amendment validates the frozen maximum and resets
   only the stopped state. Tests: `budget exhaustion survives restart, refuses new
dispatch and requires an explicit amendment`, `an ordinary provider failure
preserves its full reservation and can resume`, `interrupted streaming retains
usage...`, `fresh process resume keeps cumulative budgets and both source edits`.
3. **Bounded context from verified facts.** Context is rebuilt each turn; source
   facts are re-read and dropped if their fingerprint changed. Only receipt-backed
   summaries are used, failures/denials/uncertainties are retained, and overflow
   drops facts, then successful operation history, then older failures, while
   criteria are never dropped; `omitted` reports what was removed. Tests:
   `context contains trusted criteria and policy but no tool-role messages`,
   `context is bounded and reports omitted evidence without dropping criteria`,
   `failure fingerprints track bytes and diagnostics, never prose alone`.
4. **Pause/cancel/generation fencing and stale callbacks.** The executor checks the
   control request at safe boundaries, aborts the provider signal, records an
   interrupted/cancelled receipt, and refuses session mutations after close or a
   generation/lease change. Every provider turn and tool dispatch is raced against
   a hard, non-extendable wall-clock deadline; expiry aborts the adapter signal,
   stops further dispatch, records an `uncertain` receipt with the full
   reservation and safe-stops as `needs-attention`. The no-progress rule stops after
   three equivalent fingerprints with unchanged evidence and returns a concrete next
   action; changed bytes or diagnostics reset the count. Tests: `pause and
cancellation fence late callbacks before mutation`, `three equivalent failures
stop with an actionable reason; changed bytes remain progress`, `multi-tool
responses dispatch only complete calls and never persist a tool transcript`,
   `test evidence becomes stale after a source change and cannot complete the goal`,
   plus the deadline, ignored-abort and pause-race tests in section 5.
5. **Trusted criteria completion, including human review.** Completion is computed
   only from current artifact bytes, a successful test receipt whose dispatch-time
   source fingerprint equals the current workspace state, and an explicit trusted
   human decision for the criterion revision. The fingerprint is captured under the
   dispatch lock before the effect, over every declared artifact path and every
   journaled edit-intent path, and is re-read after the observation and again at
   completion. Provider completion text is only a summary; it never completes a
   goal. Tests: `human criteria require a trusted decision; model text never
completes`, `a model completion claim and injected policy text cannot complete or
change the goal`, and the binding regressions in section 5.
6. **DUR-003 unresolved artifacts.** Resume reconciles through the accepted recovery
   API and safe-stops on unresolved commits, workspace drift or an uncommitted tail,
   without writing or pruning. A committed edit whose cleanup is still pending also
   stops before any later effect or completion, retaining the exact artifact paths.
   Test: `unresolved DUR-003 transaction artifacts safe-stop without mutating the
workspace`.

## 5. Review repairs (2026-11-06)

Two independent review findings were reproduced before editing and repaired; an
additional same-family gap was found during self-review.

### P1 — evidence is bound to the dispatched input state

`dispatchTest` previously computed the source fingerprint _after_ `runTest`
returned. A real test of source v1 could finish, the source could become v2 before
the observation resolved, and the executor certified v2.

The executor now captures `hashWorkspacePaths(workspace, progressPaths(goal))`
inside the dispatch lock, before the intent and dispatch records are appended; the
receipt records that dispatch-time fingerprint. After a successful observation the
same path set is re-read, and the result is accepted only if the fingerprint is
identical. An unreadable re-read, an unreadable covered input at dispatch, or any
difference records an `uncertain` receipt (`test-inputs-unknown` /
`test-inputs-changed`), charges the full reservation and safe-stops
`needs-attention`; it never creates successful evidence. Completion
recomputes the same path set from the current goal and journal and requires
equality with the receipt, so a change after the receipt (including a later edit
that adds a path to the set) invalidates the pass and forces a re-test. The path
set is every declared artifact criterion path plus every journaled edit-intent
path; hash entries include existence, type, bytes and mode, so creation, deletion
and mode changes are all detected.

Where the trusted evaluator supplies snapshot evidence, `sourceFingerprint` must be
a 64-hex string equal to the dispatch-time fingerprint and `planHash`, when
supplied, must equal the dispatched plan. Mismatches record `test-evidence-mismatch`
or `test-plan-mismatch` and safe-stop. The evaluator remains responsible for mapping
its opaque plan to additional inputs outside the declared/edited set; the executor
does not infer them.

Regressions: a real child-process test that passes source v1, with the mutation to
v2 applied after the process exits but before the promise resolves; mode change,
creation, deletion, and an edit-intent path mutated during the test; an unreadable
declared input whose bytes are therefore not established; a positive control with
unchanged input and a correctly bound snapshot that still completes; a snapshot
bound to other bytes; and a snapshot bound to another plan.

### P2 — bounded tool execution

`runTest` and generic tools were awaited without a deadline: the 15 s reservation
was recorded but an over-limit adapter received no abort and still completed
successfully.

Every dispatch now races the adapter promise against a hard wall-clock
`setTimeout` and a 50 ms control poll. Expiry aborts the adapter's signal, ends the
wait regardless of whether the adapter cooperates, fences any later resolution,
records an `uncertain` receipt with `unknown-effect` and the full reservation, and
stops the loop before another call in the same response is dispatched. The provider
turn uses the same bound with `PROVIDER_MAX_MS` (30 s) and records `unknown-usage`
on timeout. A parent `AbortSignal` now also ends the wait (previously it only
aborted the child signal, so a non-cooperative adapter could still block) and an
already-aborted signal starts no effect; late aborts cannot surface as unhandled
rejections.

The executor never claims an owned process stopped because a promise timed out: the
stop reason and uncertainty state that execution may still be running, that its
result is unknown, and that the caller must verify or stop it through the DUR-005
process-ownership boundary before resuming. `toolTimeoutMs` and `providerTimeoutMs`
are trusted integration settings validated to 10 ms..policy maximum; they cannot
raise the bound, and the receipt still charges the fixed reservation, so they cannot
bypass accounting or authority.

Regressions: cooperative abort (signal observed, later call not dispatched, full
reservation charged); an adapter that ignores abort and resolves successfully after
the deadline (no second receipt, no success, goal stays `needs-attention`); a
provider that never settles; a pause race during an in-flight tool driven with
mocked timers so load cannot reorder it, followed by a fresh-process resume that
keeps cumulative budgets and completes; an already-aborted signal; and refusals for
timeout options outside the policy range. An earlier wall-clock version of the
pause race was load-sensitive under full coverage, which is why the committed
test drives the timers explicitly.

## 8. Limitations and handoffs

- **Live engine and controller integration is DUR-005.** The adapter is exercised
  with recording fake providers only. Existing engine outcomes are preserved by not
  editing `lib/model-agent.mjs`, `lib/local-agent.mjs`, `lib/controller.mjs` or their
  tests; an ownership handoff is still required before wiring this executor in.
- **Cross-process lease reclamation and approval persistence are DUR-005.** The
  accepted store has no abandoned-lease API. A real SIGKILL therefore leaves the
  persisted lease and reservation in place; a fresh process safe-stops with
  `active-attempt` rather than stealing or resetting it (verified in the SIGKILL
  test). Once a trusted reclaim releases that lease, the journaled dispatch or
  unverified marker still refuses resume with `teardown-unverified`, so reclaim
  alone cannot enable an unsafe crash-resume. Delivery of the process-ownership
  design and its durable reconciliation record is required before crash-resume
  becomes available.
- **Human decisions are trusted caller input, not yet durable.** `recordHumanDecision`
  and `options.humanDecisions` are the trusted surface; a fresh process must be given
  the decision again. Persisting approvals/decisions belongs to DUR-005/006.
- **Test freshness scope.** Test evidence binds the declared artifact and edited
  paths. The trusted evaluator owns its plan-to-source mapping for inputs outside
  that set; the executor does not infer test inputs from an opaque plan.
- **Deadline semantics are detection plus a durable block, not teardown.** A test
  or command deadline bounds the executor's wait and records conservative
  uncertainty, but it cannot kill or verify a process the adapter owns. The
  interruption therefore persists an immutable `teardown: "unverified"` marker (or
  leaves the dispatch unreceipted) and every resume refuses until the DUR-005
  process-ownership boundary attests the operation. Provider deadlines and
  failures are remote usage uncertainty and stay retryable. The executor
  deliberately does not claim the process stopped.
- **Execution-bearing kinds are `test` and `command`.** Reads and searches are
  in-process by this workflow's contract and an edit is an in-process write
  reconciled by DUR-003, so neither creates an execution block. DUR-005 must extend
  the set if it ever allows a read/search/tool adapter to own a process. A thrown
  test/command adapter error is also unverified execution: it may arrive after a
  child was spawned, so it creates the same block instead of being treated as a
  safe failure.
- **The marker is immutable.** A verified release creates the next generation but
  leaves the unverified fact in the journal, so a later resume attests the same
  operation again. DUR-005 should add a durable reconciliation record; until then
  this is conservative, not unsafe. The attestation callback must be synchronous
  and is trusted construction input, never model-reachable.
- **Timing reservations** are fixed at 30 s provider / 15 s tool active time and a
  30 s provider interrupt. These are conservative initial constants; tuning belongs
  with real-engine integration. The timeout options are bounded by those constants.
- **Not evaluated:** physical power loss, non-Linux platforms, live providers, real
  engine outcome compatibility, GUI/CLI continuation, and any cross-process
  cancellation or teardown of owned test trees.

## 10. Teardown-unverified repair (2026-11-06, unsafe-resume gap)

### Finding and reproduction

`node /tmp/matrix-dur004-recheck.mjs` on Node 24.21.0 before this repair:

```text
PASS previous source-drift case refuses completion: test-inputs-changed
FAIL unverified teardown: {"first":"needs-attention","firstReason":"tool-deadline","oldProcessAlive":true,"leaseAfterTimeout":null,"freshProcessRequests":1,"generationBefore":1,"generationAfter":2,"secondStatus":"needs-attention"}
```

A real test adapter spawned a child that outlived its 50 ms deadline. The executor
recorded `tool-deadline`, charged the full reservation, transitioned to
`needs-attention` and cleared the lease. A fresh executor process then created
generation 2 and dispatched another provider request while the owned child was still
alive (`process.kill(pid, 0)` succeeded). The receipt already said
`uncertain`/`unknown-effect`, but nothing consulted it at resume: the only
surviving-execution barrier was the lease, and every stop path deliberately released
it. The same hole existed for pause, cancellation, an adapter abort and a crash
between dispatch and receipt.

### Durable representation and ordering

The unresolved execution state is persisted as an immutable journal fact **before**
any attempt authority is released:

- The test receipt written at the stop keeps its conservative accounting and carries
  a receipt-backed summary with `execution: { teardown: "unverified", attemptId,
generation }`. `lib/goal-context.mjs` validates and preserves that field;
  `lib/goal-executor.mjs` sets it only for an execution-bearing operation that ended
  without a trusted observation (deadline, pause, cancel, adapter abort, a thrown
  adapter error or an unusable observation). The summary digest is inside the receipt's `evidence`
  array, so the marker is tamper-evident and survives process death.
- An execution-bearing operation that was dispatched but never receipted (process
  death between dispatch and observation) is itself the durable block, read through
  the accepted DUR-003 `reconcileGoalOperations`/history contract.
- Ordering: intents and dispatches are appended before the effect (unchanged), and
  `dispatchTest` appends the receipt with the marker while the lease is still held.
  Only then does the loop transition to `paused`/`interrupted`/`cancelled`/
  `needs-attention`, which releases the lease. A crash before the receipt leaves
  the dispatch record, which blocks just as durably.

Execution-bearing kinds are `test` and `command` (the durable workflow exposes no
commands yet). Provider turns are remote effects with uncertain _usage_; edits are
in-process writes reconciled by DUR-003. Neither creates an execution block, so an
ordinary provider failure or timeout remains retryable with its full reservation —
the provider-timeout regression now resumes in a fresh store and completes.

### Resume gate and the only release boundary

`begin()` — the single resume entry point — checks the durable block after the lease
check and before every terminal/budget/reconciliation decision:

- With no ownership boundary configured it returns `safe-stop` with code
  `teardown-unverified`, the exact operations (operation id, kind, attempt id,
  generation, reservation), and a handoff requirement. Nothing is appended, no
  generation is created, no provider request or tool starts and no completion is
  possible.
- With `executionOwnership.verifyTeardown` configured, the synchronous
  trusted-integration callback must return
  `{ verified: true, goalId, operations: [{ operationId, attemptId, generation,
mechanism, ownership: { pid, startTime, bootId } }] }` covering **every** blocked
  operation exactly once with matching attempt and generation. A bare
  `verified: true`, a missing identity, another generation, a missing/extra
  operation, a thrown error or an async promise is refused with the same
  `teardown-unverified` safe-stop.

No other path clears the block: elapsed time is not consulted, no PID table is
consulted, the adapter's later settlement is fenced and never read, and the model
never receives the callback, the block facts or the store. The callback is trusted
construction input in the same sense as `runTest`/`authorizeEdit`; DUR-005 must wire
it to the real process-ownership mechanism rather than to caller text.

**Precise handoff (requirement 6).** Kill, reclaim and lease reclamation are not
implemented here. DUR-005 must: persist boot id plus process start identity for each
owned test process at dispatch; verify the owned process tree is gone through a
reviewed OS ownership mechanism; return the attestation above; and add a durable
reconciliation record so an already-reconciled marker does not require repeated
attestation. Until then the block is permanent and the goal is inspect-only: no new
goal should start in the same workspace while execution may survive.

### Interruption coverage

| Interruption                                               | Durable form                                            | Release                    |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Test/command deadline                                      | receipt + `teardown: "unverified"` marker               | ownership attestation only |
| Pause while a test/command is dispatched                   | receipt + marker, then `paused`                         | ownership attestation only |
| Cancellation while in flight                               | receipt + marker, then `cancelled`                      | ownership attestation only |     | Adapter abort or thrown adapter error | receipt + marker, then `interrupted`/`needs-attention` | ownership attestation only |
| Unusable observation / protocol failure                    | receipt + marker, then `needs-attention`                | ownership attestation only |
| Crash between dispatch and receipt                         | dispatched operation without a receipt                  | ownership attestation only |
| Provider timeout / failure                                 | `unknown-usage` receipt, no execution marker            | existing retry contract    |
| Successful observation (child exited)                      | normal receipt; `process` exit is the observed boundary | no block                   |
| Observation-based uncertainty (input drift, plan mismatch) | `uncertain` receipt without an execution marker         | existing retry contract    |

### Regressions (real subprocesses and fresh processes)

- `a real test process that survives its deadline blocks fresh processes and late
success`: a real child outlives an 80 ms deadline; the receipt carries the
  `unverified` marker and the lease is released; a **fresh Node process** asserts
  `safe-stop`/`teardown-unverified` with zero requests, zero tool starts and an

- `a dispatched test without a receipt stays blocked after its lease is released`:

- `cancellation, adapter abort and a cooperative late shutdown all keep the durable

- The pause-race regression now asserts the block (and a bare attestation) before
  resuming through the attested ownership boundary, and still proves cumulative

- The `test-inputs-changed` regression asserts no execution marker is written even
  though its receipt is `uncertain`; the provider-timeout regression asserts a

## 11. Workspace-scoped durable teardown block (2026-11-06, final candidate)

### Finding and final-repair scope

The teardown-unverified repair in section 10 made the durable block correct for the
goal it was resumed against, but the executor's `durableExecutionBlock` read only that
goal's operation history. The authority boundary therefore still had a bypass family:
a fresh goal created in the same workspace (new goal id, `ready` state, generation 0,
no journal) consulted no surviving execution at all and could be admitted, dispatching
provider requests and starting edits while the original process still lived.

`node /tmp/matrix-dur004-workspace-recheck.mjs` reproduced this on Node 24.21.0 before
the final repair:

```text
PASS same-goal resume blocked: {"first":"needs-attention","firstReason":"tool-deadline","oldProcessAlive":true,"leaseAfterTimeout":null,"freshProcessRequests":0,"generationBefore":1,"generationAfter":1,"secondStatus":"safe-stop"}
FAIL same-workspace new goal edits source while old test lives: {"oldTestAlive":true,"oldGoal":"goal_ebdb639b5b634349ab2839a264282e76","newGoal":"goal_88dad0d16b3840968102dd1961359671","newRequests":2,"newGeneration":1,"status":"needs-attention"}
```

The fresh goal made two provider requests, advanced to generation 1 and edited source
while the old test process was still alive (`process.kill(pid, 0)` succeeded).

### Final repair

- `lib/goal-executor.mjs` now computes one workspace-scoped durable block:
  `blockedOperationsFor(goalId)` collects the blocked operations of a single goal
  (a dispatched execution-bearing operation without a receipt, or a receipt whose
  trusted summary carries a conservative `teardown: "unverified"` marker);
  `durableExecutionBlock(workspaceIdentity)` lists the goals bound to that canonical
  workspace identity and aggregates their blocked operations, deduplicating by
  operation id and tagging each operation with its owning `goalId`. The block is
  returned as `{ goalId, workspace: { path, device, inode }, operations }` and is
  consulted by `begin()` before any terminal/budget/reconciliation decision, before
  `beginAttempt` grants attempt authority and before any provider request, tool start
  or completion can occur. A new goal id, retry, restart, cancellation or elapsed time
  cannot bypass it because the block is bound to the canonical workspace identity and
  the original execution records.
- The accepted store needed one small additive read for this: `createGoalStore.listWorkspaceGoals`
  (documented in the store-extension bullet above) returning the goal records bound by
  canonical workspace path/device/inode; discovery runs under the caller-held
  workspace write lock, the serialization point for goal admission, so concurrent
  admission processes cannot race the check.

### Final regressions

- `a fresh goal in a workspace with unresolved surviving execution is refused while the
old process lives`: a real child process outlives an 80 ms deadline; a fresh goal in
  the same workspace is refused with `safe-stop`/`teardown-unverified`, zero provider
  requests, unchanged generation/state/lease/journal/counters and unchanged workspace
  bytes, while the old process is still alive (`process.kill(pid, 0)` ok) at the moment
  of the fresh refusal.
- `the trusted ownership attestation releases a workspace-level teardown block for a
fresh goal`: the block-bound `executionOwnership.verifyTeardown` attestation (bound to
  the fresh goal id and its blocked operation) releases the workspace block and the
  fresh goal completes.
- `an unresolved execution blocks only its own workspace`: the unrelated-workspace
  positive control completes while the blocked workspace's fresh goal is refused.
- `concurrent fresh admission attempts into a blocked workspace are all refused`: two
  concurrent fresh admissions into the blocked workspace fail closed safely (zero
  provider requests, no generation advance, no lease, journal unchanged); workspace
  lock contention surfaces as a safe-stop refusal rather than an unsafe admission.
- Store check: `workspace goal listing binds to one canonical workspace identity`.

### Final exact results

- Fresh-goal refusal while the old process lives: the fresh executor process made zero
  provider requests, refused with `teardown-unverified`, kept generation 0, state
  `ready`, lease null, journal length unchanged and counters unchanged, and left the
  workspace bytes unchanged; the old process was still alive during the refusal.
- Trusted release: the attestation bound to the fresh goal id and the blocked
  operation released the block; the fresh goal then completed with the edit applied.
- Unrelated workspace: the positive control completed normally.
- Concurrent admissions: all refused safely with zero requests and no state change.

### Final handoff and limitations

- Kill, reclaim and lease reclamation remain DUR-005; only the block-bound
  `executionOwnership.verifyTeardown` attestation may release the block, covering every
  blocked execution of the workspace. Actual process reclamation stays in DUR-005.
- Read/search adapters that secretly spawn processes are not yet in the execution-bearing
  kind set; DUR-005 must extend it if that becomes possible.
- Provider effects are not process-owned; their uncertainty remains usage-only by design.

Tested on Node 24.21.0 and 22.23.1, Linux only. Physical power loss, live providers and
non-Linux platforms remain NOT EVALUATED.
